STEP 1 — Exposure of the Gallbladder

Objective

Create optimal tension to open the hepatocystic triangle.

Instruments Visible

Arm 1 (Left hand): Fenestrated bipolar

Arm 2 (Right hand): Monopolar curved scissors or hook

Arm 3 (Right lateral): Cadiere or ProGrasp (fundus retraction)

30° down camera

± Assistant grasper (if used)

Actions

Grasp fundus with Cadiere/ProGrasp → retract cephalad toward right shoulder.

Grasp infundibulum (Hartmann’s pouch) with fenestrated bipolar → retract laterally and slightly caudally.

Adjust tension until:

Cystic plate is flattened.

Hepatocystic triangle is fully visualized.

Duodenum and colon are displaced inferiorly.

Completion Criteria

✔ Fundus under steady cephalad tension
✔ Infundibulum under lateral tension
✔ Hepatocystic triangle clearly exposed
✔ No small bowel or omentum obscuring view
✔ Adequate triangulation without arm collision

Typical Duration

2–4 minutes

STEP 2 — Dissection of the Hepatocystic Triangle

Objective

Clear fat and fibrofatty tissue from the hepatocystic triangle to isolate and skeletonize the cystic duct and cystic artery while initiating exposure of the cystic plate to prepare for Critical View of Safety (CVS).

Instruments Visible

Fenestrated bipolar (grasp + bipolar coagulation)

Monopolar hook or curved scissors (sharp/energy dissection)

Retraction grasper on fundus

Camera

Actions

Incise anterior peritoneum over the infundibulum.

Extend the incision laterally and medially along the gallbladder neck to open the anterior hepatocystic triangle.

Rotate the infundibulum medially to expose the posterior peritoneum.

Incise posterior peritoneum overlying the gallbladder neck.

Blunt sweep and bipolar coagulation to remove fibrofatty tissue and begin skeletonizing:

Cystic duct

Cystic artery

Develop a window between the cystic duct and cystic artery.

Initiate partial mobilization of the lower gallbladder from the liver bed:

Identify the gallbladder–liver interface (cystic plate).

Incise the serosa at this junction.

Dissect in the subserosal plane directly on the gallbladder wall.

Elevate the lower 25–33% of the gallbladder (infundibulum + proximal body) off the liver.

Continue until the white cystic plate is clearly visible and a posterior window is established.

Dissection must remain:

Close to gallbladder wall.

Above Rouvière’s sulcus plane.

Completion Criteria

✔ Fat and fibrous tissue cleared from hepatocystic triangle
✔ Cystic duct visually distinct
✔ Cystic artery visually distinct
✔ No structure divided
✔ Lower 25–33% of gallbladder mobilized from liver bed
✔ Common bile duct not encroached upon

Typical Duration

5–10 minutes (elective case)
Inflammation: 10–25+ minutes

STEP 3 — Achieve Critical View of Safety (CVS)

Objective

Confirm safe anatomy before division.

Instruments Visible

Fenestrated bipolar (retraction)

Monopolar hook/scissors

Fundus grasper

Camera

± Firefly fluorescence mode

Mandatory 3 Criteria

Hepatocystic triangle cleared of fat and fibrous tissue.

Lower 1/3 of gallbladder separated from liver bed (cystic plate exposed).

Only two tubular structures entering gallbladder.

Surgeon Action

Pause.

Reduce bleeding if present.

Visually inspect from multiple angles.

Confirm posterior window.

Confirm absence of additional ducts.

Completion Criteria (ALL REQUIRED)

✔ Two and only two structures entering gallbladder
✔ Posterior window visible behind cystic duct
✔ Cystic plate exposed
✔ No structure entering liver directly mistaken for duct
✔ No clip placed yet

If any doubt → do not proceed.

Typical Duration

2–5 minutes (verification phase)

STEP 4 — Control of Cystic Duct and Cystic Artery

7A — Cystic Duct Division

Instruments Visible

Robotic clip applier or assistant-applied Hem-o-lok

Fenestrated bipolar (stabilization)

Monopolar scissors (division)

Camera

Actions

Circumferentially clear duct.

Confirm adequate length.

Place:

2 clips proximally (toward CBD)

1 clip distally (toward gallbladder)

Divide between clips.

Completion Criteria

✔ Clips fully seated and locked
✔ No duct slippage
✔ No bile leakage
✔ Adequate proximal stump length
✔ Clean transection

Typical Duration

2–4 minutes

7B — Cystic Artery Division

Instruments Visible

Clip applier OR bipolar energy device

Fenestrated bipolar

Scissors

Actions

Isolate artery clearly.

Apply 2 proximal clips + 1 distal (or bipolar seal if appropriate size).

Divide.

Completion Criteria

✔ No pulsatile bleeding
✔ Clips secure
✔ No thermal injury to adjacent duct
✔ Clear separation from liver bed

Typical Duration

1–3 minutes

STEP 5 — Gallbladder Detachment from Liver Bed

Objective

Remove gallbladder from cystic plate.

Instruments Visible

Monopolar hook or curved scissors

Fenestrated bipolar (countertraction + hemostasis)

Fundus grasper

Camera

Actions

Maintain cephalad traction.

Dissect in subserosal plane.

Progress from infundibulum → fundus.

Coagulate small vessels with bipolar.

Avoid entering liver parenchyma.

If perforation occurs:

Suction immediately.

Irrigate.

Retrieve stones.

Completion Criteria

✔ Gallbladder fully separated
✔ Liver bed hemostatic
✔ No bile leakage
✔ No active oozing
✔ Cystic duct stump intact

Typical Duration

4–8 minutes (elective case)
Inflamed case: 10–20 minutes

STEP 6 — Specimen Retrieval and Final Inspection

Instruments Visible

Endoscopic retrieval bag (assistant port)

Fenestrated bipolar

Suction/irrigator

Camera

Actions

Insert retrieval bag.

Place gallbladder inside.

Inspect:

Cystic duct stump

Cystic artery stump

Liver bed

Irrigate and suction.

Remove specimen via umbilical/assistant port.

Completion Criteria

✔ Specimen fully contained
✔ No stone spillage
✔ No bile pooling
✔ Hemostasis confirmed
✔ Clips secure
✔ Clear operative field

Typical Duration

3–6 minutes
