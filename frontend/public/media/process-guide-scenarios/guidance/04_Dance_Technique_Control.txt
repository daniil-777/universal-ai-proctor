DEMO: Dance technique control - adult beginner side step and quarter turn

Principles:
- This guide describes an adult beginner demonstration clip. It is not a ground-truth label set, a performance grade or an assessment of health or balance.
- Visual observation records the visible sequence. The dancer or coach verifies comfort, practice conditions and the intended cue.
- Track the foreground dancer consistently throughout the clip. A mirror reflection is another view of the same dancer, not a separate participant or repetition. Do not double-count reflected movements; if the foreground dancer is obscured, keep the affected movement unknown.
- Show the full body, including both feet, whenever judging a visible movement sequence. Obscured feet or a cropped body remain unknown.
- Follow the coach's simple side-step and quarter-turn cue at a comfortable pace; no extreme range, acrobatics or forced movement is part of this demonstration.
- Do not infer exact joint angles, weight distribution, balance quality or unseen foot contacts from a single camera view.
- A short clip may show only one movement. Do not assume that preparation, repetitions or a final review took place off camera.
- Completion of these visual movement steps does not establish that a coach review or participant comfort check occurred; confirm those separately.

Step 1: Establish a visible starting position
Objective: Show the adult dancer and the space used for the sequence.
Tools: Clear practice area; optional floor marker; full-body camera view.
Actions:
- Stand at the starting spot with both feet visible and enough room for the planned side step.
- Face the instructor-defined starting direction and wait for the cue.
Criteria:
- Visual observation: The dancer's head, torso and both feet are in view.
- Visual observation: The dancer visibly pauses in a starting stance before the sequence.
Warnings:
- Step 1 operator verification: Adult participation, usable floor space, footwear and personal comfort require the dancer's or coach's confirmation; they are not established by appearance alone.

Step 2: Perform the visible side-step sequence
Objective: Demonstrate a simple lateral step and return using the coach's cue.
Tools: Practice area; optional starting marker; coach's side-step cue.
Actions:
- Take a comfortable side step in the cued direction and bring the other foot toward it.
- Return toward the starting spot with the same simple sequence.
Criteria:
- Visual observation: A foot visibly moves sideways and the other foot follows toward it.
- Visual observation: A return step toward the starting area is visible.
- Visual observation: Both feet remain in frame during the observed side-step sequence.
Warnings:
- Step 2 operator verification: Rhythm, intended footwork and comfort require the coach's or dancer's review; occlusion cannot support a claim about weight transfer or balance control.

Step 3: Show the cued change in facing direction
Objective: Show the direction change used for the beginner quarter-turn cue without assigning an angle score.
Tools: Practice area; coach-defined turn cue; visible starting direction.
Actions:
- Make the beginner change in facing direction previously demonstrated by the coach.
- Pause facing the new direction with the body and feet still visible.
Criteria:
- Visual observation: The dancer visibly changes facing direction from the starting orientation.
- Visual observation: A pause in the new facing direction is shown after the turn.
- Visual observation: Both feet remain visible during the observed turning movement.
Warnings:
- Step 3 operator verification: The coach confirms whether the change matches the intended quarter-turn cue and the dancer confirms comfort. Completion of this visible direction-change step does not establish an exact ninety-degree rotation or a balance score.

Step 4: Finish in a visible resting stance
Objective: End the visible demonstration with a full-body resting pause.
Tools: Practice area; full-body camera view.
Actions:
- Return to a comfortable resting stance and stop the sequence.
- Hold the finishing pause with the full body and both feet in view.
Criteria:
- Visual observation: The dancer visibly ends movement in a resting stance.
- Visual observation: The full body and both feet remain visible during the finishing pause.
Warnings:
- Step 4 operator verification: Obtain the dancer's comfort report and review the intended sequence with the coach separately. A resting pose does not prove that a debrief occurred. Do not invent a technique grade, completed repetition count or successful correction from an incomplete clip.
