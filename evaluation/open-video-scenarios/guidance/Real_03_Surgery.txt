DEMO: Real surgery video - observation of a published laparoscopic procedure

Principles:
- Reference video: real-03-surgery.mp4. This is a published, edited educational recording, not a live procedure or an instruction to perform treatment.
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Laparoscopic-treatment-of-biliary-peritonitis-following-nonoperative-management-of-blunt-liver-1749-7922-5-26-S1.ogv
- Authors: Marzano E, Rosso E, Oussoultzoglou E, Collange O, Bachellier P, Pessaux P. Published in World Journal of Emergency Surgery in 2010.
- Video license: Creative Commons Attribution 2.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/. Credit the authors and source when sharing.
- Changes: first 180 seconds of the 180.687528-second source, converted to H.264/AAC for browser playback. Original image size is 480 by 272; playback speed and aspect ratio are preserved.
- Timestamps below are approximate anchors in the delivered clip, not clinical deadlines or proof that an action occurred.
- Track the displayed view and visible working tips. Keep anything obscured, out of frame or too small to resolve uncertain.
- Distinguish a visible endoscopic image from the title, cross-sectional imaging and brief circular grayscale imaging. A cut does not establish continuous progress.
- Report visible instrument, thread, material and tube motion without inferring instrument settings, applied force, tissue identity or purpose that the view cannot establish.
- Progress means that a described visual phase has been observed. It does not establish clinical success, safety, proficiency or completion of the actual operation.
- Clinical interpretation, anatomical identification and any technique critique require qualified surgeon review and the original case context.
- There is no audible narration in this source. Do not invent spoken explanations or instructions.
- Use human review questions for uncertain contact, occlusion or abrupt changes in view. Do not present them as proven mistakes.

Step 1: Recognize the introduction and pre-procedure images
Objective: Around 00:00-00:20, identify the source introduction and imaging before the operative view begins.
Tools: Video player; playback timeline; published source.
Actions:
- Observe the opening title card.
- Recognize that the following grayscale cross-sectional images are presentation material rather than an endoscopic working view.
Criteria:
- The opening title card is visible before the operative footage.
- A sequence of grayscale cross-sectional images is visible before the first endoscopic scene.
Warnings:
- Reading an image label does not validate a diagnosis or identify anatomy. Do not mark an operative action complete from an imaging slide.

Step 2: Observe the first endoscopic instrument scene
Objective: Around 00:20-00:40, establish the first visible operative field and instrument movements.
Tools: Endoscopic video view; visible instrument tips; playback controls.
Actions:
- Follow the change from presentation images to the colored endoscopic view.
- Observe the instrument tips moving within the visible field, noting any moment when glare or occlusion prevents inspection.
Criteria:
- A colored endoscopic field replaces the introductory imaging.
- Visible instrument tips move in the operative field during this section.
Warnings:
- Only visible tips and motion are established. Tool function, tissue identity, force and a successful treatment result are not determined from this scene.

Step 3: Follow the visible thread and instrument sequence
Objective: Around 00:40-01:55, observe thread or loop handling and repeated instrument movements in the edited close-up sequence.
Tools: Endoscopic view; visible thread or loop; visible working tips.
Actions:
- Identify the visible thread or loop when it enters the view.
- Follow the shown working tips and changes in thread position through the sequence.
- Treat each cut or obscured interval as a break in evidence.
Criteria:
- A thread or loop is visible together with working instrument tips.
- The thread position changes while a visible tip handles or approaches it.
- Repeated instrument movements are visible in the close-up sequence.
Warnings:
- A visible loop or pull is not proof of a completed secure knot, an adequate repair or absence of tissue injury. Do not prescribe a suture method or infer hidden contacts.

Objective: Around 01:55-02:05, distinguish the brief circular grayscale image and the subsequent return to the colored field.
Tools: Video player; playback timeline; visible imaging views.
Actions:

- Identify the return to a colored endoscopic view without assuming continuous action across the cut.
Criteria:
- A circular grayscale imaging view appears between operative scenes.
- The displayed video returns to a colored endoscopic field after that view.
Warnings:
- This checkpoint establishes a view change, not a diagnostic interpretation, imaging result or completed clinical decision.

Objective: Around 02:10-02:38, follow the white woven material and instrument handling in the visible field.
Tools: Endoscopic view; visible woven material; visible instrument tips.
Actions:

Criteria:
